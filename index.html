A perfect woman

Click anywhere or press the button to begin.

But don’t forget

    Author's Note

    I wrote this poem a couple of months ago when I was feeling very overwhelmed with trying to juggle all the responsibilities of being a wife, a student, a teacher, a daughter, a sister, and a friend, all while maintaining my mental and physical health. I have always been passionate about gender studies and about the societal expectations/norms set for women. I was reflecting on my childhood, and about how I was raised to believe that I could do anything I set my mind to. However, the harsh reality is that it is hard to balance chasing your dreams while still maintaining a personal life. This is a reality that is universal and experienced by all genders, but I feel it is especially difficult for women. The poem is very personal to me, as I included specific quotes and messaging from my personal life. For example, growing up, I never liked to wear makeup. My grandma, who raised me, would always tell me that I should “put on a little lipstick” because I looked sloppy without it. This advice always confused me, because she would praise my “natural beauty” in the same breath. This is just one example of the contradictory messages I felt I received as a young girl. I hope that my piece captures the mixed messages that society sends to young women.

    My vision in adapting this poem to a piece of e-literature was to make it look as though a young woman was taking notes in her diary about how to achieve perfection as a female. I attempted to capture the difficulty and frustration a young, impressionable girl might feel when trying to do everything just right. There were a few changes I had to make to capture this vision in my e-literature piece. My original poem had both parts of each line on the same page. For example, “has natural beauty... / but puts on a little lipstick.” However, I ended up placing the second part of each line on a new sheet of paper in my e-literature piece to emphasize the dichotomy of each sentiment. In addition, my original poem was titled “Barbie.” I ended up changing this to “A Perfect Woman” to suggest that the speaker is a young woman making a list in her diary. I also added the “But don’t forget” title to the second page to provide a contrast between the two parts of each line. Finally, I added the final ellipsis and ending passage to show the speaker’s frustration and confusion. The ending passage is from another poem I wrote that has similar themes to the one I have adapted for this e-literature piece. I believe this passage acts as a sort of thesis statement for the piece as a whole.

    I created this e-literature piece using vibe-coding. I started using Microsoft Copilot. Having the poem appear on a piece of notebook paper with hand-written font and a pink background was relatively simple. However, adding the sound effects was a great deal more difficult than I had anticipated. I initially used the synthesized audio that Copilot provided for the pencil taps when ellipsis appeared. I wanted a more realistic sound, however, which required me to record my own voice memo. I did this for the pencil-tapping sound, the pencil-scribbling sound, the page-flipping sound, and the paper crumple sound. Trying to upload these simple voice memos into Copilot was a headache. No matter what format I put the recordings into, Copilot would not accept them. At this point in the process, I switched to ChatGPT. Chat was able to embed my audio-clips with very minimal difficulty. I spent some time trying to get the pencil-tapping synchronized with the ellipsis and the pencil-scribbles to sound right, but it was definitely an easier process than with Copilot. It took a few prompts to get the page-flips flowing the way I wanted and at the pace I wanted, but I am relatively happy with the end result. I did have to ask Chat to add a “start animation” button to get the simulation to play. On both Copilot and Chat, I tried to prompt AI to make the paper crumple look realistic. I tried to be very detailed, but what appears on my e-literature piece is as realistic as I was able to get it to look.

    Overall, I believe my ‘Signal and Surface’ project shows frustration and confusion in a way that my words on a piece of paper could not. I love that I was able to simulate the writing process of a young girl struggling to be what everyone expects of her. I was able to take my poem and create an entire story around it using technology. This piece has been adapted beyond what was shown in class as I have made the sound effects louder. I wanted the sounds to be amplified because sound is an integral aspect of the mood of this piece. I also added a trashcan for the balled up paper to be thrown into to signal exasperation and defeat on the part of the narrator.
